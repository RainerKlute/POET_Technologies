#  Business Update with POET Tech's Chairman and CEO

2026-09-30

- Video: <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cEEQjIpm0WU>

**Adrian Brijbassi:** In August, POET Technologies stated it would have announcements in September. Chairman and CEO Dr. Suresh Venkatesan joins me here to provide a statement. Suresh, we’re at the end of the month. What can you tell us?

**Dr. Suresh Venkatesan:** First, thanks, Adrian. This is the update referenced in August. We said that we would make additional announcements. And the updates in September are subject to our confidentiality agreements. I’ll tell you where the work stands and why some of it doesn’t have names on it yet.

Honestly, our heads have been down, really focused on work. That’s not the same as having nothing to say. We’re executing rapidly and working cohesively as a team to deliver for our customers.

**Adrian Brijbassi:** And shareholders are eager to know about manufacturing. What can you tell us about where things stand on that subject?

**Dr. Suresh Venkatesan:** In August, we said we expected to begin shipping a substantial number of production units for qualification in the remaining quarters of 2026. We’re doing that. Production units are with customers for qualification now, built on our existing capacity, some of which were legacy that we inherited from our joint venture in Super Photonics.

The fab we have previously announced in Penang, we’re planning to scale that from approximately a million units a year this year to about a factor of 10 more than that rate over the coming years. And that’s unchanged. We’re actually executing to that as we speak. And it’s a well-funded plan, especially since our balance sheet supports it.

We’ve placed about 48 million dollars in purchase orders for production equipment against that plan already. And we’re working diligently with our suppliers to have them scheduled for delivery starting this month and into the first quarter of next year. Installation will be in Q1. Most of this equipment will be used for production starting Q2 of next year to support a relatively rapid ramp in terms of unit shipments.

We’re now producing products in the thousands across 400, 800, 1.6T for both sampling, qualification as well as customer acceptance testing. With new technology, there’s always a latent fear that it’s not going to be manufacturable or how quickly can it be scaled. I can affirmatively state that we’re past what one might call teething problems when it comes to new technology. And we’re now able to manufacture the products with good yields.

We’ll definitely have further to report on that with our third quarter results that we expect to publish on November 16.

**Adrian Brijbassi:** That’s really exciting details there, Suresh. Can you tell us now who are these units going to? And you mentioned you were going to not mention the names, but is there any other details you might be able to be specific on?

**Dr. Suresh Venkatesan:** Let’s see, our customers asked us not to divulge their names until our product is qualified in their systems. And you can imagine why that is the case because in some cases we’re working on custom technology for them. And they’d like to keep it that way. That’s pretty normal in our industry. People typically don’t announce suppliers before a qualification is done.

Our progress occurs in four stages. I think we’ve mentioned this before. You start with sampling, go through qualification, then there’s customer acceptance, and then commercial shipment. Today, depending on the product, we’re either in sampling qualification or customer acceptance across multiple customers.

At our annual meeting, we said we had more than 10 active engagements, which can cumulatively result in over $100 million of annualized revenue. Nothing’s changed there. We continue to work diligently towards meeting those customer requirements and expectations and capitalizing on what we think is significant momentum behind us since the early part of this year.

Announcing names can come when qualifications are completed and if the customer at that point in time chooses to disclose it. But outside of that, we’re definitely bound by our confidentiality terms.

**Adrian Brijbassi:** One name we’ve been hearing a lot about is Lumilens. Where does the Lumilens relationship stand?

**Dr. Suresh Venkatesan:** I think as we discussed before and have announced, Lumilens has placed an initial order for parts from POET for $50 million under a framework that can reach over $500 million over five years. One of the largest purchase orders we’ve had as a company and we’re really as a company heads down focused on executing towards this.

The requirements call for several new high-speed designs utilizing the tools and the toolkit that POET has developed over these past many years. Much of the design work has been completed and product validation is expected to start soon. So we’ll report obviously continued progress related to that order in future updates.

**Adrian Brijbassi:** Now, you and several members of the team were at CIOE and ECOC this month. What did you see that tells you where the market is going and where POET fits in that market?

**Dr. Suresh Venkatesan:** The industry has decided copper is coming out of the rack and light is going in. Every conversation at the CIOE and the ECOC was more about how fast is that shift actually going to take place. I think we’ve been hearing about the rise of pluggables, the growth of NPO, CPO, and it’s always been 2027, 2028.

I think the consensus is that late 2027 ramp, but really the first major year of NPO kind of taking root, if you will, in the industry is 2028. The bottleneck is moving to light sources and packaging, which is exactly where our Optical Interposer sits.

I’d said earlier that we’ve got a lot of momentum behind us since earlier this year, starting at the OFC, and it’s because of this. We’ve got technology in our portfolio that can help with the scale. We’re well poised and incredibly busy really with the opportunities we’re working through.

The market we’re building for is roughly 20 billion by 2030 by LightCounting estimates. This is not a bubble. This is a major trend from a semiconductor perspective. You have the cloud, you have the PC era, and this is big. The whole push towards more optics driven by, of course, AI.

The other good thing is that our Blazar continues to gain significant attention as an external light source with really game-changing capabilities for the industry. We’re very excited about that. We have sponsored programs for that product, and we’re just heads down, really focused on execution at this point in time because that’s all that matters. The opportunities are there. We just need to grab them and we’ve got every means in our arsenal to be able to do so.

**Adrian Brijbassi:** Part of grabbing those opportunities might mean going quiet now and then as you focus on the work, and shareholders have written to the company saying that when POET does go quiet, the stock lags. What do you have to say about that?

**Dr. Suresh Venkatesan:** Look, I think they’re right. Quiet has a cost and I loaned that. Since this spring, we’ve been focused on execution and really reinforcing three Cs that I’d established as kind of a mantra for our workforce, capability, credibility, capacity.

We’re bound by confidentiality on the most interesting parts of the work that we’re doing. Neither of those is a reason not to tell you what we can, when we can, and that’s what this update is meant to provide.

**Adrian Brijbassi:** What happens between now and the third quarter results in November, and when exactly do shareholders get to hear about them next?

**Dr. Suresh Venkatesan:** The third quarter results will be announced in November. In that report, you’ll see qualification progress against our plan. More importantly, our capacity build. Probably the largest single tranche of capacity expansion that we’ve so far done as a company. I think it’s an important data point for our shareholders.

Of course, between now and then, if there’s news to announce, we will share that through our usual news channels.

**Adrian Brijbassi:** Now a topic I know you’re really passionate about, the team you’re building. POET has gone from about 115 employees to 155 over the course of the year so far. What does that say about the trajectory of the company?

**Dr. Suresh Venkatesan:** We’re now more than 155 people across six countries. Of course, much of the growth here is in Asia and Singapore, which is where I am right now. We started with a small office, about 2000 square feet or so. We’ve moved to a much larger office across the hall.

We’re building a full new prototype line here in Singapore so we can not disrupt the high-volume manufacturing in Penang for new products. The team is really expanding.

These employees are essential for our achievements. With such growth, it’s often difficult to make sure we have a tight-knit team that everybody knows the roles they’re playing. It’s one of the reasons why we convened earlier this year, the first ever POET Day in Singapore in August. It gave us a chance to gather in one place as a team, lay out our collective and individual goals.

The theme of the conference was the road less traveled because a lot of what we’re doing, we find like it’s not been done before and we’re having to chart our own path and trajectory towards success. Team building events like that are great for camaraderie and overall company performance.

It was quite energizing for the entire team and invigorating for me personally to see how far we’ve come. I remember back in 2017, we were five people. It’s been steady, it’s been good progress and I think there’s more growth to come.

**Adrian Brijbassi:** For me, observing POET Day, it was a very motivational experience. I could tell how attentive the group was and how energized they were to be in a position to propel POET to the next level and the next level after that. The question is five weeks after that team gathering, are you seeing the cohesion you wanted and are you happy overall with the progress?

**Dr. Suresh Venkatesan:** There was clearly an opportunity to galvanize the team on solving immediate problems. It’s amazing you get a team like that together all in one room. Things that have remained unsaid get said. We can take action on either questions or issues people might have.

I’m happy to say we’ve completed a lot of necessary tasks in the past few weeks. We’ve implemented processes across the company that are intended to make us more seamless, more efficient.

We’ve hired data scientists into our organization that can use AI tools to improve our productivity, to improve visibility into our status of manufacturing. That’s really made a lot of difference.

As a company, we’ve adopted AI tools that are being used now across a lot of the tasks that otherwise would be less efficient. It was great because it brought the company together and we were able to now leverage on the skills across the different sites that we have.

**Adrian Brijbassi:** That’s a great update. Thank you for it and for continuing to progress the company.

**Dr. Suresh Venkatesan:** Thank you, Adrian, and thank you to all our shareholders. We’re building something that very few companies in this industry are positioned to build. We now have marquee customers engaged across both our high speed and our high power segments, a technology roadmap that is as aggressive and as competitive as anything in this market.

Light is replacing copper across the data center. That shift is worth roughly 30 billion by the end of the decade. And POET does sit at the pinch point where that transition has to happen in both the light sources as well as the packaging.

That’s what our team of more than 155 people across six countries is executing on right now. I really like where this company stands today and I like it even more at where it’s headed.
